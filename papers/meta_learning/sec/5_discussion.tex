\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The current evidence supports a focused interpretation: meta-learning improves the SpiderMark verifier, especially under downstream transformations that are poorly captured by static supervised training.
This does not mean that the watermark pattern alone changed.
The central comparison should be against the original SpiderMark verifier and against SOTA/canonical meta-learning baselines that could plausibly explain the same gains.

This distinction matters for the final paper narrative.
If MetaSpiderMark is presented as a new meta-learning algorithm, reviewers will expect comparisons with meta-learning methods such as MAML variants, Reptile-style updates, or broader few-shot learning baselines.
If it is presented as a robust watermark verification framework, the paper still needs to show that its meta-learning choice is competitive under realistic downstream attacks.
The benchmark should therefore prioritize SpiderMark anchor baselines and a matched scheduler table, while treating the SOTA/canonical meta-learning table as the context that explains whether the verifier update itself is competitive.
In practice, this means first completing the fixed-budget scheduler rows for UCB, ATS-style, BASS-style, and ASr-style sampling, then adding MAML, ANIL, Reptile, Matching Networks, Prototypical Networks, and an R2D2-style ridge solver under a fixed scheduler.

There are also practical limitations.
Meta-training adds computational cost because each episode requires support and query passes, and checkpoint selection can materially affect downstream robustness.
The residual and LLM-assisted schedulers introduce additional degrees of freedom; they should be evaluated carefully so that the paper does not conflate verifier meta-learning with scheduler sophistication.
For the main paper, ATS-style adaptive task scheduling, BASS-style contextual bandit scheduling, and ASr-style adaptive sampling are the most important SOTA-inspired scheduler baselines because they directly target task selection during meta-learning.
The paper should avoid overclaiming exact reproduction unless the original ATS or BASS code is ported into this benchmark.
The LLM-assisted scheduler is better framed as a diagnostic extension unless it provides a large reproducible gain over ATS, BASS, and the local residual scheduler.
Finally, all preliminary results in this draft should be considered provisional until repeated across seeds and evaluated on the full benchmark suite.
