Reliable diffusion-watermark verification must survive compression, cropping, resampling, geometric distortion, and compound platform processing. SpiderMark combines structured frequency-domain watermarking with a learned verifier, but fixed perturbation mixtures cannot adapt to changing attack difficulty. We propose \emph{Agentic MetaSpiderMark}, which uses online verifier feedback to schedule attack-conditioned meta-learning episodes while leaving the watermark and diffusion model unchanged. Because deployed verification requires a binary decision, we prioritize fixed-threshold accuracy and report AUROC as a secondary metric. In a matched comparison of six schedulers on an unseen 1,000-image COCO test set, Agentic MetaSpiderMark achieves the highest mean accuracy across eight attacks: 89.15$\%$ (95$\%$ bootstrap CI: 88.06--90.21). Paired bootstrapping confirms positive gains over uniform sampling, Adaptive Sampler, and Bandit-UCB. BASS and ATS have lower mean accuracy but remain statistically competitive. Uniform slightly leads AUROC (96.00$\%$ versus 95.86$\%$). Agentic MetaSpiderMark is therefore the strongest accuracy-first scheduler in this controlled benchmark, rather than a universal winner under every metric.
