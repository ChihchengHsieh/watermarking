\begin{abstract}
Reliable watermark verification for diffusion-generated images must remain stable after the image has passed through unknown downstream transformations, including compression, cropping, resampling, geometric warping, and compound platform-style processing.
SpiderMark addresses this problem with structured frequency-domain watermark injection and a learned verifier, but the verifier is still trained from a fixed distribution of perturbations and checkpoints.
This paper extends SpiderMark by formulating verifier training as a meta-learning problem.
Rather than optimizing the verifier only for the current mini-batch, the proposed framework constructs watermark verification episodes from attack-conditioned support and query sets, performs an inner verifier adaptation step, and updates the verifier using query loss so that it learns parameters that transfer across downstream attacks.
We further evaluate checkpoint variation and define a task-scheduler benchmark that compares uniform sampling, bandit scheduling, ATS-style scheduling, BASS-style scheduling, and ASr-style adaptive sampling under matched verifier episodes.
Preliminary experiments on nine image transformations show that the meta-learned verifier improves mean accuracy from 0.8499 to 0.9182 and mean AUROC from 0.9293 to 0.9686 over a non-meta SpiderMark verifier, with gains on every evaluated attack.
The largest observed improvement occurs under compound messaging-app processing, where accuracy rises from 0.6813 to 0.9173.
These results suggest that robust watermark verification is not only a matter of watermark geometry or verifier architecture, but also of how verification episodes are constructed and scheduled during meta-training.
\end{abstract}
