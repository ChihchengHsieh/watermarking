Reliable watermark verification for diffusion-generated images must remain robust to unknown downstream transformations, (e.g., compression, cropping, resampling, geometric distortions, and compound platform processing). Existing methods, such as SpiderMark, combine structured frequency-domain watermarking with a learned verifier, but their training relies on fixed perturbation distributions and static curricula. We propose \emph{Agentic MetaSpiderMark}, an agentic meta-learning framework that adaptively schedules watermark verification tasks during training. The proposed agent monitors online verifier signals, including query loss, uncertainty, failure rate, and sampling history to dynamically prioritize attack-conditioned meta-learning episodes. Unlike fixed augmentation strategies or hand-crafted curricula, Agentic MetaSpiderMark closes the loop between verifier performance and attack selection while leaving the SpiderMark watermark and diffusion model unchanged. We further introduce a benchmark comparing the proposed scheduler against uniform sampling, bandit scheduling, ATS, BASS, and Adaptive Sampler under identical training conditions. Experiments on nine downstream transformations demonstrate consistent improvements over the original SpiderMark verifier, increasing mean verification accuracy from 84.99$\%$ to 91.82$\%$ and AUROC from 92.93$\%$ to 96.86$\%$, with gains across all evaluated attacks. The largest improvement is observed under compound messaging-app processing, where accuracy increases from 68.13$\%$ to 91.73$\%$. These results demonstrate that adaptive agentic task scheduling is a key ingredient for robust watermark verification, complementing advances in watermark design and verifier architectures.
