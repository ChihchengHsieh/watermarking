\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper draft extends SpiderMark from a static learned verifier to a meta-learned verifier trained over downstream attack episodes.
The approach keeps the original SpiderMark watermark injection mechanism while changing how the verifier learns to generalize across perturbations and checkpoints.
Preliminary results show consistent gains over a non-meta verifier across nine transformations, suggesting that verifier training strategy is a key component of robust diffusion watermarking.
The next step is a controlled scheduler benchmark that evaluates uniform sampling, UCB bandits, ATS-style adaptive scheduling, BASS-style contextual bandits, and ASr-style adaptive sampling under matched attacks, checkpoint rules, and compute budgets.
After the scheduler comparison is stable, a fixed-scheduler SOTA/canonical meta-learning comparison should evaluate FOMAML, MAML, ANIL, Reptile, Matching Networks, Prototypical Networks, and an R2D2-style ridge solver.
