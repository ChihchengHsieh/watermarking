Reviewer’s comments to authors: |---
This paper builds on watermark channel estimation for resisting screen-shooting (D/A/D conversion) to propose an architecture that:
1. uses skip connections to build a wavelet-type filter bank with down-/up-sampling capability (seeking clearer separation between analysis
scales);
2. uses attention-based Laplacian enhancement of textures (where most watermark energy is usually carried).
The experimental part shows clear improvement, although maybe not as significant as the Authors claim, compared to the SOTA using
neural nets. The respective merits of the two modules are assessed using sound ablation studies. In my opinion, the improvement is better
overall robustness on more attacks, not so much the 0.52dB reduction in the embedding distortion.
I’d suggest only minor editing:
- I guess the word ’wavelet’ has to appear somewhere, as the resulting architecture clearly builds on the same structure;
- I don’t see any reason why the venerable Bit-Error-Rate (BER) should be replaced by the ’Bit Accuracy’ that the authors promote, as BER
= 1 - BA and watermarking is fundamentally a variation on communications with side information.

Coming back to the results, what they suggest is:
- better modelling capability of the network when it can learn the wavelet filters (this is what these approaches change fundamentally
compared to the classical perfect-reconstruction, bi-orthogonal framework);
- it is still unclear whether these techniques outperform the results obtained, e.g., by Deguillaume and Pun (SPIE, 2002) with the classical,
much more energy-friendly and distortion-controlled schemes dating back 20+ years ago (that can embed 64 bits too, but at 38dB and
resisting roughly the level and variety of attacks considered by the Authors).

Response to Reviewer 1
We thank the reviewer for the positive assessment.
Response to Reviewer 2
2.1 Role of PIMoG and comparison with PixelSeal/TrustMark
In our work, PIMoG is not the main novelty but a training component used to approximate the compound degradations of screen-shooting.
The main contribution is the cascaded dual-UNet-based watermarking framework and the high-frequency enhancement channel attention
module.
PixelSeal and TrustMark are important references for conventional geometric and electronic-channel distortions, but such robustness does
not transfer to screen-shooting, which also introduces illumination changes, blur, moire, and device artifacts. Our reproduction shows that
PixelSeal outperforms TrustMark. In its original setting, PixelSeal achieves 93.8%, 95.0%, and 96.3% under JPEG-50, Cropout-0.3, and
resize-0.4, with a PSNR of 36.75 dB. However, when directly evaluated under the screen-shooting scenario, its performance is limited. At a
shooting angle of 20° and a shooting distance of 50 cm, its decoding BA is only 84.3%. After retraining it under the same resolution,
payload, and PIMoG-based noise layer as our method, its BA under screen-shooting improved to 95.8% at the same shooting setting, but its
PSNR decreased to 34.19 dB, and its Cropout-0.3 robustness also declined markedly. This indicates a trade-off between screen-shooting
robustness, visual quality, and conventional-channel robustness. Our method shows more stable performance across shooting angles,
distances, and devices.
2.2 Security implication of Fig. 3
Fig. 3 illustrates the embedding structure rather than a directly observable fixed watermark template. Message recovery relies on learned
decoding, not direct reading of a visible pattern. We will clarify that our work focuses on robustness under screen-shooting conditions,
while cryptographic security analysis is outside its scope.
Response to Reviewer 3
3.1 Relation between WNet and wavelet-type filter bank structures
We thank the reviewer for this point. WNet shares a multi-scale analysis-reconstruction characteristic with wavelet-type filter bank
frameworks. However, the method does not use a classical wavelet transform. The analysis and fusion processes are learned through
convolution. The backbone is a learnable cascaded dual-UNet with two-stage encoding/reconstruction and dense cross-stage skip
connections. The relation is structural rather than based on a wavelet-filter design.
3.2 BA and BER
We used BA for consistency with recent neural watermarking papers and because it directly reflects the proportion of correctly decoded bits.
The two metrics are equivalent: BER = 1 - BA. We will make this relation explicit in the revision.
3.3 Discussion of Deguillaume and Pun (SPIE, 2002)
Deguillaume and Pun is a representative classical method with strong affine synchronization capability. However, our work focuses on the
screen-shooting setting rather than general affine robustness. Since the task assumptions and evaluation protocols are not fully aligned,
direct comparison would not be rigorous. We will narrow the claim to \223under the considered screen-shooting scenario\224.
3.4 Discussion of traditional watermarking methods
Following the reviewer\222s suggestion, we examined traditional methods represented by Pun et al. However, some implementation details
could not be recovered reliably from the public descriptions, so we could not obtain stable results for a fair comparison. We also examined
classical transform-domain baselines such as the DWT-DCT-SVD family. Although these methods are effective under standard digital
distortions, their robustness can not transfer reliably to the screen-shooting protocol. This is because screen-shooting involves the
display-camera pipeline, which alters photometric response and sampling structure. In this case, traditional schemes designed primarily for
conventional digital-channel distortions may have limited robustness in a screen-shooting scenario.
